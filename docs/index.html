Customers without power, statewide

How well does it predict real outages?

Every model run is scored against EAGLE-I, ORNL's record of customers out by county every 15 minutes. The model was calibrated jointly on 22 wind and rain events from 2018–2025, the largest in the EAGLE-I record, excluding ice and snow storms and reporting glitches. The two 2024 events are independent tests, run with ERA5 (hindcast) and with NOAA GFS forecasts issued 1–3 days ahead.

What we learn

    How this goes beyond EAGLE-I

    EAGLE-I is the best record of US outages: it shows who is out now, which is why it serves here as ground truth. This system adds the decisions that come before and during an event:

    The two work together: EAGLE-I's 2014–2025 archive provides hundreds of Kentucky events to train and verify the forecast model on, and its live feed can update the restoration forecast as an event unfolds.

    Method

    Weather inputs

    • Hindcast: ERA5 reanalysis, hourly 10 m gusts and rain.
    • Forecasts: NOAA GFS runs issued 1, 2 and 3 days before each valid hour (Open-Meteo Previous Runs API, available from early 2024).
    • Kentucky is sampled on a 0.25° grid plus every county centroid (288 points).

    Outage model

    D = 1 − (1 − Fgust)(1 − Frain 24h)(1 − Frain 72h)

      The county outage share U tracks the -hour mean damage. It is restored with time constant τ = τ₀(1 + R₇₂/ mm), with work paused while gusts exceed km/h or rain exceeds mm/h. Customers out = U × customers (EAGLE-I modeled customer counts).

      Limitations & next steps

      • One set of gust fragility curves can't fit every event: on ~25 km ERA5, some windstorms with similar peak gusts caused 10× fewer outages than March 2023, and thunderstorm days barely register. Next, replace the curves with a learned model using gust exposure area, vegetation, terrain, feeder exposure and utility territory.
      • Thunderstorm (convective) gusts are invisible at 25 km. Use HRRR (3 km) and Aircast SR (1 km) inputs.
      • EAGLE-I doesn't cover every Kentucky customer (some co-ops report incompletely), so observed outages are a lower bound.
      • Transmission-level failures and generation shortfalls (e.g. the December 2022 TVA rolling blackouts) are out of scope.
