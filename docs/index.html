Would we have seen it coming?

Each forecast row runs the same outage model on NOAA GFS forecasts issued 1, 2 or 3 days before each hour, and compares the result with the ERA5-driven hindcast. A county is "flagged" when its peak outage is ≥ % of people.

This measures consistency with the reanalysis hindcast, not accuracy against real outages. We haven't found a public, county-level record of unplanned outages for Kenya. Validation needs KPLC interruption logs; see the Method tab.

Known gap: Nairobi

How this goes beyond EAGLE-I

ORNL's EAGLE-I collects outage counts from US utilities every 15 minutes and maps customers out by county. It's an excellent situational awareness tool: it shows what is out now. This prototype aims at the decisions that come before the outage and during the restoration:

The two approaches are complementary. A Kenyan EAGLE-I-style feed of KPLC outage counts would be the ideal training and validation target for the forecast model.

Method

Weather inputs

  • Hindcast: ERA5 reanalysis (hourly rain and 10 m gusts), our best estimate of what happened.
  • Forecasts: NOAA GFS runs issued 1, 2 and 3 days before each valid hour (Open-Meteo Previous Runs API), i.e. what a forecaster would actually have had.
  • Every county is sampled at ~0.5° grid points plus its centroid (199 points nationally).

Outage model

At each point, lognormal fragility curves turn weather into the share of customers whose supply is interrupted:

D = 1 − (1 − Fgust)(1 − Frain 24h)(1 − Frain 72h)

    County outage share U tracks the 6-hour mean damage and is restored with time constant τ = τ₀(1 + R₇₂/ mm). Restoration pauses while gusts exceed km/h or county-mean rain exceeds mm/h (crew safety). People affected = U × county population (2019 census).

    Limitations & next steps

    • Fragility parameters are expert priors, not yet calibrated. With KPLC interruption logs we will fit them, then replace them with a learned model (gradient boosting / graph neural network over the feeder network).
    • Reanalysis and GFS at ~25 km miss intense local storms (see Nairobi). Aircast SR at 1 km / 1 hour is designed to fix this.
    • Population is used in place of electricity customers; county electrification rates differ widely.
    • River flooding, landslides and grid-level cascading failures (e.g. transmission trips causing national blackouts) aren't modelled.
